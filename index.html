04 / WHY IT MATTERS

Less rediscovery.
More continuity.

The value is not another generated paragraph. It is a team that can pick up the thread—and understand where it came from.

Qualitative intended outcomes.
No measured ROI or time-saving claims.

  1. 01

    Pick up where the team left off

    A shared brief helps reduce repeated rediscovery when account ownership or meeting attendance changes.

  2. 02

    Keep the reasoning within reach

    Source traceability makes the evidence behind a summary inspectable, rather than asking the next person to trust a detached conclusion.

  3. 03

    Make handoffs safer

    Preserve unresolved questions and opportunity states. A new team member should inherit the caveats, not just the headline.

  4. 04

    Build reusable decision evidence

    Account context can support the next brief, the next question, and the next human decision without losing its source trail.

05 / SCOPE & CONFIDENCE

Useful context.

THIS PUBLIC SHOWCASE

Conceptual, by design.

Presentation code and recreated abstract examples only. All interactions are canned and run locally in your browser. No live AI, CRM access, analytics, or external requests.

No customer records, private screenshots, tenant identifiers, workflow exports, or prompt source are included. This is not the solution implementation.

HISTORICAL EVIDENCE

Previously verified paths.

Saved historical verification covers email and posted Teams summary deduplication, account briefs, all three query modes, a published agent, and targeted evaluation.

These are historical checks—not newly checked tenant behaviour, broad reliability guarantees, or production certification.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS

Not every path is live-verified.

  • Newly recorded native Teams transcript ingestion has not been live-verified.
  • Oversize content is currently rejected. Long-transcript chunking is future work.
  • Scheduled ingestion and brief rebuilding are not instantaneous.
  • Accurate aggregate counts do not mean every source is in a bounded evidence sample.
  • AI output still needs human review. Access and privacy gates remain essential.
